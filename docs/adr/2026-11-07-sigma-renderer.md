# The Map is drawn by Sigma.js with whimc's own WebGL programs

The Map is rendered with Sigma.js 3 over a graphology graph, with two programs of our own: a disc for Modules (lens shading, a rim that outlines a level shown as context, a Churn glow, a focus or Changes ring, a hollow ghost) and a line for Dependencies (solid for runtime, dashed for type-only, dotted for dynamic). A hand-written WebGL renderer on regl or three.js was the alternative the spec left open; Sigma was kept because it already gives what a hand-written one would have to rebuild — camera with wheel, pinch and drag, animated camera moves, GPU picking of the hovered node, a label grid on a 2D canvas, buffers updated per changed node — and custom programs leave the look entirely to us.

The renderer stays thin: the view state decides what to draw (`computeView`), pure modules turn its decisions into attributes and scales (`web/src/render/attributes.ts`, `scales.ts`, `camera.ts`), and `graphSync` applies only what changed to the graph. Two things live on the GPU so that zooming and hovering never rebuild the buffers: the level blend is a uniform read every frame (the view state is recomputed only when the camera changes which levels are drawn or what is fetched, see `cameraSignature`), and opacity changes are transitions the shader interpolates from a start time. Node sizes are in map units (`itemSizesReference: "positions"`), so the engine's radii are drawn as they are, with a minimum radius on screen so small Modules stay visible and clickable.

Accepted costs: Sigma's label grid does not know label widths, so whimc drops colliding labels itself; sigma ignores node attributes it does not know, so every visual decision is an attribute of ours that only our programs read.

## Dependencies run from disc to disc, above the discs

Sigma stacks Dependencies under the Modules, and the Map packs large discs close together: the heaviest coupling of a monorepo (two big Packages side by side) ran entirely under their discs and could not be seen. Dependencies are now drawn above the discs and under the labels (CSS order of sigma's canvases), trimmed in the shader to run from one disc's boundary to the other's, so that a Dependency never covers its own ends; between discs that touch or overlap, it is drawn as a 16 px stub across the point where they meet. Their width grows quadratically with their strength within the level, and their opacity with it, so that the few heavy couplings stand out from the many light ones. Rejected: arcs around the discs (they cross more of the Map), and a minimum length alone with Dependencies kept under the discs (the stub would still be hidden between overlapping discs).

## A parent fades out as its children are revealed, highlighted or not

The fade a highlight spares a Module (focus, search match, a change) is handed over as the level below it is revealed: the view state blends a node's fade towards the fade of a Module nothing singles out (`contextFade`), and the shader does the same per frame from the reveal of the level below (`u_childReveal`). The highlight survives as the ring (drawn at the level's weight or 0.8 of the reveal, whichever is more) and the badge. Sigma's redraw of the hovered node over every other is turned off, as it put a hovered parent over its children.

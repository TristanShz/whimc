# The Map is drawn by Sigma.js with whimc's own WebGL programs

The Map is rendered with Sigma.js 3 over a graphology graph, with two programs of our own: a flat disc for Modules (a quiet tint of its Churn step with a crisp 1 px outline, the outline kept for a level shown as context, a ring for the focus, a search match or a Changes mark, a dashed outline for a ghost) and a thin line for Dependencies (solid for runtime, dashed for type-only, dotted for dynamic). A hand-written WebGL renderer on regl or three.js was the alternative the spec left open; Sigma was kept because it already gives what a hand-written one would have to rebuild — camera with wheel, pinch and drag, animated camera moves, GPU picking of the hovered node, a label grid on a 2D canvas, buffers updated per changed node — and custom programs leave the look entirely to us.

The renderer stays thin: the view state decides what to draw (`computeView`), pure modules turn its decisions into attributes and scales (`web/src/render/attributes.ts`, `scales.ts`, `camera.ts`), and `graphSync` applies only what changed to the graph. Two things live on the GPU so that zooming and hovering never rebuild the buffers: the level blend is a uniform read every frame (the view state is recomputed only when the camera changes which levels are drawn or what is fetched, see `cameraSignature`), and opacity changes are transitions the shader interpolates from a start time. Node sizes are in map units (`itemSizesReference: "positions"`), so the engine's radii are drawn as they are, with a minimum radius on screen so small Modules stay visible and clickable.

Accepted costs: Sigma's label grid does not know label widths and dropped the labels of mid-sized discs next to big ones, so whimc draws the labels itself after each render (every disc of 9 px of radius or more on screen, forced ones first, then the level in view, biggest first; a label that collides takes the place above its disc, else is left out); sigma ignores node attributes it does not know, so every visual decision is an attribute of ours that only our programs read. The label is our `text` attribute, not sigma's `label`, so that sigma does not index every label in the grid it no longer uses.

## Keeping it fluid with tens of thousands of items

Measured on a 5.9k-File repository (25k Dependencies): sigma reindexes the whole graph for every node or edge dropped, so leaving the Files level dropped thousands of items one by one and froze the page for up to two minutes. The rules that came out of it:

- Nothing is dropped one by one. What leaves the view fades out (and shrinks a little) and stays in the graph, invisible, until it outnumbers the rest; then, or when many items arrive at once, `syncGraph` hands back a new graph that sigma takes in one pass (`setGraph`). Many changed items are updated in one event, with the changed attributes as the hint, so that sigma only places items again when their position, size or order changed.
- At most 3,000 Dependencies are drawn per level: every one singled out (focus, Changes, ghost), then the strongest (`edgeBudget`). Past that a level's Dependencies are a texture, and drawing them cost frames.
- The view state is recomputed when the levels drawn change, at once, and when the area a big level is fetched for changes, only once the camera has rested 160 ms; a pan with no level fetched by area recomputes nothing.
- Labels: each frame visits only the discs large enough on screen (`LabelIndex`, sorted by size per level), places them in a grid of cells (`LabelBoxes`), and stamps them from an atlas of pre-rendered labels (text and halo set once, at most a few new ones per frame) instead of setting text on the canvas every frame.

Nothing on the Map animates on its own: a frame is drawn only when the camera, the data or a fade changes. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, arriving nodes fade in without scaling.

## Dependencies run from disc to disc, above the discs

Sigma stacks Dependencies under the Modules, and the Map packs large discs close together: the heaviest coupling of a monorepo (two big Packages side by side) ran entirely under their discs and could not be seen. Dependencies are now drawn above the discs and under the labels (CSS order of sigma's canvases), trimmed in the shader to run from one disc's boundary to the other's, so that a Dependency never covers its own ends; between discs that touch or overlap, it is drawn as a 16 px stub across the point where they meet. Their width takes one of three discreet steps from their strength within the level (0.75 px to at most 2.5 px), and their opacity a little with it. Rejected: a minimum length alone with Dependencies kept under the discs (the stub would still be hidden between overlapping discs).

Each Dependency barely bows to the left of its direction (6 % of its length), so that A→B and B→A take two lines instead of one. Direction shows only where it is asked about: the Dependencies of the hovered or selected Module narrow towards the imported Module (to 35 % of their width), rather than every line tapering or carrying an arrowhead, which clutter a dense level. Stubs stay straight. Wide arcs around the discs stay rejected: they cross more of the Map.

## A parent fades out as its children are revealed, highlighted or not

The fade a highlight spares a Module (focus, search match, a change) is handed over as the level below it is revealed: the view state blends a node's fade towards the fade of a Module nothing singles out (`contextFade`), and the shader does the same per frame from the reveal of the level below (`u_childReveal`). The highlight survives as the ring (drawn at the level's weight or 0.8 of the reveal, whichever is more) and the badge. Sigma's redraw of the hovered node over every other is turned off, as it put a hovered parent over its children.

## A sober visual language, its tokens defined once

The first look (domed shaded discs, a breathing bloom, tapered arcs, a violet-to-orange ramp, a dot grid with a vignette) read as decoration. The Map now follows the restraint of tools like Linear or Vercel, dark theme first: a near-black ground with no grid; flat discs, a low-alpha tint of their Churn colour and a 1 px outline in it, no glow, shadow or pulse; Dependencies in neutral grey at about a fifth of full opacity; Inter for every label, the hierarchy between levels carried by weight and tone (Packages 13/600, Directories 12/500, Files 12/450 and quieter), Geist Mono only for paths. Interaction states stay plain: the focus is a brighter ring with its neighbours kept and the rest dimmed, a search match a ring in the accent colour, added and modified Modules a green or amber ring, a removed one a dashed red outline, changed-File counts a neutral pill.

Churn is quantised into five steps (`churnStep`), from a calm grey-blue to amber, evenly spaced in OKLab, so that a colour on the Map is one of the few the Legend lists, each with the commit counts it covers at the level in view (`churnRanges`, from the same log scale the view state normalises with). Size stays the radius and Churn the colour.

Every colour is a CSS custom property in `style.css`, defined once as a `light-dark()` pair; `data-theme` on the root forces a theme (the viewer's choice, kept in browser storage), and without it the system decides. The renderer reads the same tokens (`readPalette` resolves each through a probe element, since a custom property keeps `light-dark()` unresolved) and reads them again when the theme changes, so the WebGL palette and the chrome never drift apart. Spacing (4–24 px), radii (4/6/8 px), type sizes and motion (150 ms and 220 ms, eased) are tokens there too.
